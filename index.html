Single-Photon Optical Communication

Ongoing

Research · National research lab · Sept 2026 – present

Free-space optical communication in the photon-starved regime — where the receiver counts individual photons rather than measuring a continuous optical power. Detecting single photons is what makes an optical link work across distances and power budgets where a conventional receiver would see nothing at all, which is why it underpins deep-space optical communication.

  • Optical links at this sensitivity live or die on pointing: the beam is narrow enough that holding the terminal on target is as much of the problem as the detector is.
  • That constraint is what the two-axis gimbal below is being built for — a direct-drive pointing platform with no gearbox backlash in the loop.

Transcutaneous Spinal Stimulation

Ongoing

Researcher · Casa Colina Hospital · Feb 2026 – present

A custom transcutaneous spinal cord stimulation device for stroke rehabilitation. When a stroke disrupts signalling between the motor cortex and the spinal cord, some neural drive often survives but is too weak to move the muscle. The device aims to detect that residual impulse, amplify it, and re-emit stimulation directly to the muscle — closing the loop the injury broke, and promoting neural pathway regrowth through repetition.

  • Assemble and calibrate the device, diagnosing and resolving hardware faults alongside a PhD researcher.
  • Support experiments varying pulse duration, period and amplitude to find stimulation parameters that recruit muscle reliably without overwhelming the recorded signal.
  • Work on device performance and data quality — surface EMG at these amplitudes sits close to its own noise floor, and the stimulation artefact is orders of magnitude larger than the signal you are trying to recover.
  • Preparing for clinical patient trials at Casa Colina, summer 2027.

Inverted Pendulum

Complete

Controls lab design project · mechanical design, firmware, controls

A 750 mm cart-pole system built from scratch, balanced upright by a linear-quadratic regulator running across two microcontrollers on a CAN bus.

  • CAD'd the rail, cart and bearing assemblies, sourced the parts and hand-soldered the CAN protoboards.
  • Distributed control architecture: a cart-side Teensy 4.0 reads pendulum angle from an AS5600 magnetic encoder over I²C and transmits it at 600 Hz over a CAN bus to a Teensy 4.1, which closes a 600 Hz loop and commands an ODrive S1.
  • Chose LQR over PID — a cart-pole has four states and one input, and LQR coordinates them through a single optimal gain matrix instead of four fighting loops.
  • Firmware state machine with four modes: IDLE, MANUAL homing on a trapezoidal trajectory, ARMED position-hold, and BALANCING.

Two-Axis Camera Gimbal

Design study

Mechanical design, motor control · supports the optical communication research

A direct-drive two-axis pointing platform for the optical communication work above — built for precise tracking, not handheld stabilisation. Currently a costed design: parts are selected, nothing is ordered.

  • Direct-drive BLDC on both axes — no gearbox, so there's no backlash to fight in the pointing loop.
  • Specified around paired ODrive S1 controllers, M8325s 100 KV motors and Jetson Orin Nano compute, with CAN between them.
  • Blocked on payload mass against cantilever load — the constraint that sets every other number in the design, and the reason nothing is ordered yet.
