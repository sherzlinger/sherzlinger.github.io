Researcher · Casa Colina Hospital · Feb 2026 – present

Transcutaneous Spinal Stimulation

Ongoing

A custom transcutaneous spinal cord stimulation device for stroke rehabilitation. When a stroke disrupts signaling between the motor cortex and the spinal cord, some neural drive often survives but is too weak to move the muscle. The device aims to detect that residual impulse, amplify it, and re-emit stimulation directly to the muscle — closing the loop the injury broke, and promoting neural pathway regrowth through repetition.

  • Assemble and calibrate the device, diagnosing and resolving hardware faults alongside a PhD researcher.
  • Rebuilt and improved a custom isometric strength-testing rig that synchronizes force sensors and linear encoders with a 7-channel EMG through a DAQ, so muscle activation can be cross-referenced against force output across seven muscle groups.
  • Support experiments varying pulse duration, period and amplitude to find stimulation parameters that recruit muscle reliably without overwhelming the recorded signal.
  • Work on device performance and data quality — surface EMG at these amplitudes sits close to its own noise floor, and the stimulation artifact is orders of magnitude larger than the signal you are trying to recover.
  • Preparing for clinical patient trials at Casa Colina, summer 2027.
